Exit and profit-taking rules

How to read

Two common trailing-stop rules and two volatility-based targets per position. The tight stop is the 22-day high minus 2× average true range (ATR, the average daily trading range); the wide stop uses 3×. The targets are one and two monthly standard deviations above today's price. On a random walk the odds of touching them within a month are about 32% and 5%. "Gain kept at wide stop" is the share of your current paper profit you would keep if you sold at the wide stop or today's price, whichever is lower.

How aggressive traders use these numbers

General practice, described so you can judge it. It's not a plan for your account.

  • Size by risk, not by dollars. Many active traders cap each position's share of risk rather than its share of money, so one volatile name can't dominate the account. Compare the two bar charts above.
  • Scale out into strength. A common pattern sells a fixed slice at the +1σ target and trails the rest. The backtest above shows the trade-off: much smaller worst falls, but lower returns on most tickers, because stops also sell you out of rebounds.
  • Let trend decide re-entry. Many systematic funds only add when price is above both the 50- and 200-day averages, and treat "Downtrend" names as off-limits for new money.
  • Treat leverage as rented. Leveraged ETFs lose to decay in sideways markets, so traders usually hold them for days or weeks with a hard exit, not as long-term positions.
  • Keep dry powder. Aggressive books often hold 10–20% cash so a sharp sell-off is an opportunity rather than a forced sale.

Model validation: does the loss forecast hold up?

How to read

Each day of the last two years, three methods forecast tomorrow's loss threshold for your current holdings using only earlier data. A breach is a day the actual loss went past the forecast. A good 99% forecast is breached about 1 day in 100. Kupiec's test checks the count; Christoffersen's checks whether breaches bunch together. A p-value under 0.05 fails. The Basel traffic light is the bank regulators' scorecard: 0–4 breaches of the 99% line in 250 days is green.

Method and limits
  • Positions and cost basis come from your two Robinhood accounts, synced Nov 21, 2026. Prices are Friday Nov 20 regular-session closes. Robinhood's own total includes after-hours trades, so it reads slightly higher.
  • The two-factor stress chart applies Nasdaq and Bitcoin sensitivities in log terms, so leveraged funds compound. On last month's real moves it understated the loss, which is why the 24-factor model, the six loss methods and the 500-day backtest were added. Trust the methods that pass the backtest.
  • Factor model: 24 ETF-proxy factors, EWMA-weighted ridge regressions over two years (half-life one year), EWMA factor covariance (half-life about three months). BMNR uses data only from July 2025, when it became an Ether treasury company; earlier days are filled from its factor fit.
  • The exit-rule backtest includes trading costs and next-day execution but not taxes. Selling winners in a taxable account can cost more than the backtest shows.
  • The NVDX call enters the factor model at its delta-equivalent exposure; in the two-factor stress chart it's assumed worthless in down scenarios.
  • Tax lots and holding periods aren't included, and they matter for any real profit-taking decision.
